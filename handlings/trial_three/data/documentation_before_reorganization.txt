# Third handling — temporal resolution

## Why this stage exists

Stage 2 established persistent assembly for 30/30 reference random-start runs within 180 s, no censoring and no post-confirmation breakups over the observed follow-up. Checkpoint restoration was exact. Those findings remain evidence for that specific reference protocol, not for any new timestep or turning formulation.

The remaining issue is temporal resolution before the future speed range reaches 5 BL/s. At high speed, coarse updates can cross the repulsion range between samples. Meanwhile, smaller dt in the instantaneous-turn model also allows more complete social direction changes per simulated second. Thus dt changes numerical encounter resolution and the implemented behavioural response.

No prior evidence was overwritten, no model classes were duplicated, and no light was added. All notebooks import the existing [shared model](../../shiner_model.py) and the streamed observer helper from Stage 2. The bounded option already existed; this stage reuses it and strengthens validation rather than silently changing defaults.

## Hypotheses

**A — instantaneous turning:** further refinement might produce sufficiently similar aggregate behaviour. Test dt=.03125/.015625/.0078125, speeds1/3/5, ten matched seeds and equal31.25-s horizons.

**B — bounded social turning:** a fixed physical angular capacity might give a more interpretable response and better practical stability. Test 14 rad/s at dt=.0625/.03125/.015625/.0078125, with the same speeds, seeds and physical duration. Both arms retain 360° FOV, zero angular noise, the same local social radii, synchronous updates and reflecting walls.

Puckett reports 1.75 rad per update at dt=.125. Dividing by .125 to obtain **14 rad/s**, and scaling the cap as `14 * dt`, is our explicit implementation convention, not a fitted literature rate. Hard-wall reflection remains a separate instantaneous response outside that social cap.

## Predeclared practical criteria

The [criteria JSON](data/predeclared_criteria.json) was saved before the experiments. Primary decisions compare matched-cohort **ensemble means** for adjacent dt values:

- Polarization, rotation, largest-group fraction and connected-time fraction: absolute change ≤.05.
- MeanNND and mean distance to centroid: relative change ≤10%. Near-zero distance references use the declared absolute fallback, not an unstable division.
- Both candidate and finer mean missed-pass rates ≤.01/model/s; zero sampled exact overlaps in the two cohorts.
- A candidate must pass at all three speeds and every finer available adjacent comparison. This avoids choosing an accidental agreement at one pair.

Raw values, paired-seed absolute changes and rate trends are reported. Paired trajectories are not required to be identical: long trajectories can be sensitive. These are project decision conventions, not biological constants, statistical significance tests or a formal convergence theorem. Ten seeds and one finite horizon limit inference.

The common output interval is .125 s and the late metric window is the last12.5 s. Whole-horizon connected-time fraction and every-update encounter/assembly diagnostics remain distinct from these late averages.

## Results

### Implementation is correct in the controlled tests

All **12/12 checks** passed. The `max_turn_rate=None` path preserves previous trajectories, records and RNG states exactly. Wraparound and clockwise/counterclockwise clipping work. Total maximum social turning is 14 rad over one physical second at all five tested dt values.

At internal dt=.015625, recording every eighth update gives .125-s output. Over 640 internal updates, full recording has641 group rows versus81 sparse rows. Every internal state, retained group/fish row and RNG state matches exactly. Recording cadence is not the source of the dynamics differences.

### Hypothesis A: instantaneous turning does not pass

The finest comparison is .015625 → .0078125:

| Speed (BL/s) | Relative change in mean centroid distance | Additional failed criteria |
|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 37.08% | Connected-time fraction: absolute change .07410 |
| 3 | 52.31% | P .06393; R .05095; connected-time fraction .052675 |
| 5 | 27.37% | None additional |

No instantaneous candidate passes across all speeds. Missed passes/model/s across .03125 → .015625 → .0078125 are 0→0→0 at speed1, .0192→.0096→0 at speed3, and .0928→.0160→0 at speed5. Zero detected finest-step encounters does not make the aggregate measurements stable.

Thirty overlapping conditions were freshly rerun and exactly reproduce nine Stage-2 measurements, supporting cross-stage consistency without reusing those runs as the new cohort.

### Hypothesis B: bounded turning also does not pass

For .015625 → .0078125:

| Speed (BL/s) | Failed finest-pair criteria |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Mean centroid distance: 2.225382→2.546543 BL, relative change12.61% |
| 3 | All criteria pass |
| 5 | Mean centroid distance: 1.063108→1.383269 BL, relative change23.15%; connected-time fraction .822100→.659875, change .162225 |

Only1/9 complete bounded speed/pair comparisons passes, and no candidate satisfies the all-speed chain rule. At speed5, bounded missed-pass rates decrease **.304→.0416→.0032→0** across the four timesteps. Bounded turning has a clear physical rate and improves some comparisons, but it is not a uniform solution to practical temporal sensitivity.

All cases have zero sampled exact overlaps. Nevertheless the minimum sampled pair separation anywhere in the bounded cohort is only .00380 BL. The model uses point-like agents and soft behavioural repulsion; zero exact coincidences and the restricted straight-path detector are not a hard-body collision-avoidance guarantee. Reflected segments are excluded from missed-pass counts.

## Final social protocol

**No new protocol is accepted.** The existing deterministic reference remains available, but neither temporal formulation is promoted to an official light protocol. No accepted internal dt is assigned merely because it is the smallest tested.

Following the conditional rule, the final30-seed×180-s cohort was **not run**, and **zero checkpoints were regenerated**. These are untested gates, not evidence of failed assembly under the two new formulations. The previous 30/30 assembly result is not transferred automatically. All earlier checkpoint files remain untouched and retain their stored protocol.

The numerical and response formulation needs a further decision before a light-ready protocol can be selected. Possible contributors include hard social-zone switches, boundary events and finite-cohort/trajectory sensitivity. These experiments measure the disagreement but do not isolate its cause. A focused localization and uncertainty analysis is preferable to silently relaxing thresholds, tuning cohesion parameters, or assuming that another halving will necessarily resolve it.

## Light readiness

**NOT READY FOR LIGHT.**

- Controlled turning, backwards compatibility and recording independence: passed.
- Practical temporal stability across speeds1–5: not passed.
- Final new-protocol assembly/stability cohort: not executed, because the prerequisite failed.
- Official regenerated checkpoints and restoration for that protocol: not executed.

The machine-readable decision is [04_final_social_protocol.json](data/04_final_social_protocol.json). No LightField, light measurement, speed modulation, environmental noise or gradient-performance metric was implemented. The study stops at this result for review.
